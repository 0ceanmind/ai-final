SEARCH STRATEGIES

How AI navigates problems.

Two families. Uninformed search is blind. Informed search uses a heuristic to peek ahead.

UNINFORMED
BFS
FIFO queue · level by level

Explores all nodes at the current level before going deeper. Guarantees the shortest path in steps. Memory hungry.

UNINFORMED
DFS
Stack · depth first

Goes as deep as possible before backtracking. Very low memory use. Can get stuck in infinite loops.

INFORMED
Greedy Best First
f(n) = h(n)

Always picks the node that looks closest to the goal. Fast but not optimal.

INFORMED
A* Search
f(n) = g(n) + h(n)

Combines actual cost with heuristic estimate. Optimal if the heuristic is admissible (never overestimates).

The four properties to compare any search algorithm Completeness · Optimality · Time Complexity · Space Complexity
LOCAL SEARCH

Climbing the solution landscape.

→ SIMPLE
Hill Climbing

Always move to a better neighbor. Easy to implement, easy to trap in a local optimum.

→ THERMAL
Simulated Annealing

Inspired by metallurgy. Allows occasional worse moves to escape local optima, with decreasing probability.

→ PARALLEL
Local Beam Search

Tracks multiple states at once. More exploration, less risk of getting stuck.

Networks that learn.

An ANN is a web of artificial neurons inspired by the brain. Inputs become outputs through weights, summation, and a final activation function.
SIDE BY SIDE

Biological meets artificial.

VISUAL · MULTI-LAYER PERCEPTRON
The three layer structure
1943. McCulloch and Pitts propose the first artificial neuron. Binary inputs, binary output, no learning. Every modern deep network descends from this one model. The origin moment
TYPES OF NETWORKS

Pick your flavor.

→ FORWARD
Feedforward

Data flows one way: input to output. No loops.

→ IMAGES
Convolutional (CNN)

For grid-like data like images. Uses convolution and pooling layers.

→ SEQUENCES
Recurrent (RNN)

Has feedback loops. Built for sequential data like text and time series.

→ DISTANCE
Radial Basis

Activations based on distance from a center point.

→ DIVIDED
Modular

Independent sub-networks combined at the end.

LEARNING RULES

Six ways a network updates itself.

i
Hebbian

"Neurons that fire together wire together." Correlation strengthens weights.

ii
Perceptron Rule

Adjust weights based on the error between predicted and actual output.

iii
Delta Rule

Gradient descent. Minimizes squared error. Foundation of modern training.

iv
Competitive

Only the winning neuron updates. Used in clustering and self-organizing maps.

v
Reinforcement

Weights change based on rewards or penalties for actions.

vi
Oja's Rule

A modified Hebbian rule that normalizes weights to prevent unlimited growth.

SUPERVISED LEARNING

Four famous networks.

Trained with labeled data. The lineage runs from Rosenblatt's Perceptron all the way to today's deep learning.

1958FRANK ROSENBLATTLINEAR
Perceptron

The simplest neural network. Single layer, step activation function, linear classifier. Works only for linearly separable problems.

WIDROW & HOFFCONTINUOUSMSE
Adaline · Adaptive Linear Neuron

Like the Perceptron but with a continuous activation function. Minimizes Mean Squared Error.

Outputy = Σ wᵢxᵢ
Delta Rule for weight updateΔwᵢ = η · (d − y) · xᵢ

d is desired, y is predicted, η is the learning rate.

MULTI ADALINENON-LINEAR
Madaline · Multiple Adaline

Multi-layer extension of Adaline. Handles non-linear problems.

Two training rules: Rule I updates weights only when the output is wrong. Rule II minimizes a global cost function.

1986RUMELHART, HINTON, WILLIAMS
Backpropagation

The workhorse of deep learning. Four phase training loop:

1. Forward Propagation · compute outputs from inputs
2. Error Calculation · compare prediction to target
3. Backward Propagation · send error backward
4. Weight Update · adjust with gradient descent
ASSOCIATIVE MEMORY

The Hopfield network.

1982JOHN HOPFIELDRECURRENT
Recall from partial input

A recurrent network used as associative memory. Stores patterns and retrieves them even from noisy or incomplete inputs. Recalling a face from a blurry photo is the classic example. Also used to find energy minima in optimization problems like the Traveling Salesman.

THE FOUR PHASE PIPELINE

How an FLC thinks.

01

Fuzzification

Convert crisp input into fuzzy membership values. 25°C becomes 0.3 cold, 0.7 comfortable.

02

Rule Base

A set of IF THEN rules. IF temp is cold, THEN fan is low. The expert knowledge lives here.

03

Inference Engine

Evaluates the rules and aggregates them into one combined fuzzy output set.

04

Defuzzification

Convert that fuzzy output back into one crisp number the real world can act on.

DEFUZZIFICATION METHODS

Six ways to crisp it up.

i
Centroid Method

Finds the center of mass of the fuzzy output set. The most common method.

ii
Bisector of Area

Divides the area under the membership curve into two equal halves.

iii
Mean of Maximum (MOM)

Average of all values where membership is at its peak.

iv
Largest of Maximum (LOM)

Largest value where membership reaches its peak.

v
Smallest of Maximum (SOM)

Smallest value where membership reaches its peak.

vi
Weighted Average

Average of outputs weighted by their degrees of membership.

Optimization by imitation.

When the search space is too big to brute force, AI borrows from nature. Genetic Algorithms mimic evolution. Ant Colony Optimization mimics foraging swarms.
Nature spent four billion years optimizing. The smartest algorithms are the ones that copy her homework. Why nature inspired algorithms work
GENETIC ALGORITHM

Survival of the fittest solution.

Treat each possible solution as a chromosome. Evolve a population of them over generations. The best survive and reproduce.

01 · INIT
Initial Population

Generate a random set of candidate solutions, each encoded as a chromosome.

02 · SCORE
Fitness Evaluation

Score each chromosome using a fitness function. Higher fitness means a better solution.

03 · PICK
Selection

Pick parents for the next generation. Fitter chromosomes have a higher chance of being chosen.

04 · MIX
Crossover

Combine genetic material from two parents to produce offspring with mixed traits.

05 · TWEAK
Mutation

Randomly flip small parts of a chromosome to maintain diversity and avoid local optima.

The three core operators every GA uses Selection · Crossover · Mutation

ANT COLONY OPTIMIZATION

Following the pheromones.

Real ants find shortest paths to food without a map. They drop pheromones, the shortest path accumulates the most pheromones, and the whole colony converges on it.

i
Pheromone Trail

A chemical scent ants drop on their way. Stronger scent equals more attractive path.

ii
Probabilistic Choice

Each ant picks its next step probabilistically based on pheromone strength plus heuristic info.

iii
Evaporation

Pheromones fade over time, preventing the colony from getting stuck on suboptimal paths.

iv
Reinforcement

Better solutions get extra pheromone. The colony converges on the shortest path.
